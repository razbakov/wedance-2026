# Jobs to Be Done — Meetup Planner

Jobs exist independent of WeDance. They describe what dancers are trying to accomplish around festivals — whether they use this product, WhatsApp groups, or ask strangers at the hotel breakfast.

## How to Read This

Each job follows the JTBD format:

> **When** [situation], **I want to** [motivation], **so I can** [expected outcome].

Jobs are grouped by persona. Struggling moments capture the real-world triggers that create urgency around each job.

---

## Festival Dancer

*A dancer who buys a ticket to a multi-day festival in another city. Attends 2–6 festivals per year. Travels alone or with one friend.*

### Job 1: Coordinate travel to the festival

> **When** I've bought my festival ticket and need to figure out how to get there, **I want to** find other dancers traveling from the same area, **so I can** split the cost and not drive 6 hours alone.

Most dancers decide on festivals 2–4 weeks ahead. Transport is the first logistical pain — flights are expensive, driving alone is boring, and trains don't always go where festivals are. Today this is solved by posting "anyone driving from Berlin?" in WhatsApp groups and hoping for a reply.

**Struggling moments:**

- Scrolling through 3 different WhatsApp groups looking for someone who mentioned a carpool, finding nothing
- Booking a solo rental car because no one replied to my "anyone driving from X?" post in time
- Arriving at the festival and discovering 4 other people drove from the same city separately

### Job 2: Find affordable accommodation with compatible people

> **When** I'm looking for a place to stay during the festival, **I want to** find roommates who match my budget and habits, **so I can** split the cost and actually sleep.

Accommodation is the biggest cost after the ticket. Sharing an Airbnb with 3–4 dancers cuts costs by 70%, but finding compatible roommates (budget, sleep schedule, gender preference) through WhatsApp is chaotic. Bad matches lead to awkward festivals.

**Struggling moments:**

- Paying €120/night for a hotel solo because I couldn't find roommates in time
- Sharing a room with someone who parties until 5am when I need sleep for morning workshops
- Seeing a post "looking for 1 more person for our Airbnb" too late — it was posted 3 days ago in a group I muted

### Job 3: Meet new people beyond the dance floor

> **When** I arrive at a festival where I don't know many people, **I want to** have structured ways to meet others, **so I can** make real connections instead of just dancing with strangers.

The best festival memories happen between workshops — dinners, beach trips, late-night conversations. But breaking into groups is hard. Dancers eat alone, scroll their phones between sessions, and go back to their room. The dance floor is social; everything around it isn't.

**Struggling moments:**

- Eating dinner alone at a restaurant near the venue because I don't know anyone well enough to suggest "let's go together"
- Standing in the lobby during a 2-hour break with nothing to do and no one to do it with
- Going home after a festival and realizing I danced with 50 people but made zero actual friends

### Job 4: Find dance partners that match my level

> **When** I'm at a festival with hundreds of dancers, **I want to** find partners who dance my style at my level, **so I can** enjoy workshops and socials without the awkwardness of mismatched partnerships.

At a 200-person festival, maybe 30 dance your style at your level. Finding them means trial and error on the dance floor. Followers who want intermediate bachata leads have no way to find them except by dancing with everyone. Workshop partner selection is pure luck.

**Struggling moments:**

- Sitting out a workshop because the only available partner is way above or below my level
- Dancing the entire social with beginners because I couldn't find the intermediate dancers in the crowd
- Wanting to practice a specific style but not knowing who else at this festival dances it

## Festival Regular

*An experienced dancer who attends 6+ festivals per year. Knows many people in the scene. Naturally organizes group activities and connects people.*

### Job 5: Organize group activities without becoming a full-time coordinator

> **When** I'm going to a festival and people ask me "what's the plan?", **I want to** coordinate rides, rooms, and dinners without endless back-and-forth, **so I can** enjoy the festival instead of managing a spreadsheet.

Festival regulars become de facto organizers — friends ask them for carpool seats, roommate intros, and restaurant picks. This is flattering until it becomes a logistics nightmare across 5 WhatsApp threads, 3 Google Sheets, and 20 DMs. They want to help but not manage.

**Struggling moments:**

- Spending 2 hours on WhatsApp coordinating a dinner reservation for 8 people who keep changing their minds
- Running a shared Google Sheet for the Airbnb split and chasing 3 people for their payment
- Feeling responsible when someone shows up and has no one to hang out with, because you forgot to include them

### Job 6: Share local knowledge with visiting dancers

> **When** the festival is in my city or a city I know well, **I want to** share restaurant recommendations, transport tips, and hidden gems, **so I can** help visitors have a better experience and earn social capital.

Locals and frequent visitors have insider knowledge — which restaurants are great near the venue, which taxi apps work, where the unofficial after-party is. Today this knowledge lives in their head or gets shared one-on-one in DMs. There's no way to broadcast it to all attendees.

**Struggling moments:**

- Answering the same "what's a good restaurant near the venue?" question from 10 different people
- Knowing a great spot for group dinner but having no way to invite all 200 festival attendees
- Watching visitors get ripped off by tourist-trap restaurants when there's a local gem around the corner
