# Requirement: Festival Schedule Access

**Actor:** Dancers, Festival organizers
**Domain:** Festival Experience

## Purpose

### Driver
Festival organizers promote the same event across multiple channels individually, with limited visibility into reach or impact. Dancers receive schedules as static images (printed, PDF, Instagram) that cannot be filtered, shared, or updated — leading to missed workshops and coordination friction.

### Requirements

**Dancers** need clear, filterable, and shareable access to festival workshop information so that they can plan their schedule and coordinate with friends.

**Organizers** need visibility into workshop demand and actual attendance patterns so that they can optimize room planning, adjust capacity, and allocate resources.

## Priority
High — first experiment per organizational strategy

## Type
Operations

---

## Experiments

Two sequential experiments testing different paths to fulfilling the requirements above.

### Experiment A: Dancer-driven (B2C)
**Hypothesis:** Dancers will use a digital schedule if someone converts the static one — without organizer involvement.

**How it works:**
1. Get a festival's published static schedule (PDF, Instagram, flyer)
2. Convert it into a digital interactive version on WeDance
3. Share the link in the festival's WhatsApp/Facebook group
4. Measure usage

**What dancers get:**
- Filter by style, room, time
- Personal plan — mark workshops, see your day at a glance
- Share with friends — coordinate without WhatsApp chaos
- Community updates if organizer changes rooms/times

**Success metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Trigger (pivot threshold) |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| Dancers who open the schedule | 20%+ of attendees | Below 10% → rethink distribution channel |
| Return visits during festival | 3+ per dancer | Below 1.5 → schedule alone isn't enough value |
| Organic shares | Dancers share link without prompting | Zero shares → no viral loop, rethink the hook |

**Risks:** Lower data quality (depends on community accuracy), harder to monetize without organizer relationship.

### Experiment B: Organizer-driven (B2B)
**Hypothesis:** Festival organizers will partner with WeDance if it gives them data they can't get elsewhere (workshop demand, gender balance, attendance) — at no risk to their existing ticketing.

**How it works:**
1. Organizer partners with WeDance — keeps their existing ticketing
2. Shares the WeDance link with ticket holders: "Plan your festival here"
3. Dancers sign up — browse schedule, mark workshops, find partners
4. Organizer gets analytics dashboard

**What organizers get:**
| Feature | Value |
|---------|-------|
| Gender balance data | Lead/follow ratio per workshop, before it starts |
| Workshop demand signals | Adjust rooms and times based on real interest |
| Attendance analytics | Actual participation, not just ticket sales |
| Promotion package | WeDance promotes festival to dancers from similar events |
| Less support load | No more "where's the schedule?" DMs |

**Pricing (to be validated):**
| Tier | Price |
|------|-------|
| First festival | FREE — case study and proof of concept |
| Per-festival fee | TBD — after validation |
| Promotion packages | TBD — cross-sell future festivals to past attendees |

**Success metrics:**
| Metric | Target | Trigger (pivot threshold) |
|--------|--------|--------------------------|
| Pilot partner sign-up | 1 organizer | No interest after 5 pitches → rethink value proposition |
| Dancer adoption via organizer link | 30%+ of ticket holders | Below 15% → organizer distribution isn't enough |
| Organizer NPS | 8+ | Below 6 → product doesn't solve their real pain |
| Repeat partnership | Organizer signs up for next festival | No repeat → one-time novelty, not a habit |

**Risks:** Harder sales cycle, organizer may see WeDance as competing with their ticketing.

### Experiment sequence
Test B2C first (lower barrier). If dancers use it, approach the organizer with usage data to bridge into B2B.

## Notes
Target: multi-day dance festivals (salsa, bachata, kizomba) with 100+ attendees. Start with one festival where we have connections.
