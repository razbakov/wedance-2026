## What Social Dance TV is

Social Dance TV (SDTV) is a premium media and positioning business in the social dance world.

We are not just a videography service.
We are not just a social media page.
We are not just content creators.

SDTV operates as a **festival media and positioning partner**.

Our work sits across 3 value layers:

1. **Production**
   High-quality on-site video and photo coverage for festivals, artists, and dance events.

2. **Distribution**
   Publishing and amplifying content through SDTV channels and collaborations, so the content actually reaches people and creates visibility.

3. **Positioning**
   Helping festivals, artists, and events look stronger, feel bigger, and gain more authority, social proof, desirability, and momentum.

This distinction is critical.
Agents must not think about SDTV as "someone filming dance videos."
The real business value is: **capture the event, distribute the energy, strengthen the brand.**

## Event-phase logic

### Pre-event
Hype, anticipation, ticket desire, artist visibility, momentum building.

### Live event
FOMO, real-time energy, social proof, community engagement, visibility spikes.

### Post-event
Proof, nostalgia, brand reinforcement, recap value, organizer satisfaction, dancer re-engagement, sales opportunities.

### Renewal period
Keep relationship warm, show value delivered, reopen for next edition, upsell visibility and support, move from one-off to recurring partnership.

Each phase has a different business purpose. Never treat "event media" as one single blob.

## Client types

### Primary: Festivals / organizers (main B2B growth engine)
Care about: event image, turnout and excitement, brand prestige, artist presentation, media consistency, feeling bigger online, reliable execution, easier promotion, long-term visibility.

### Secondary B2B: Artists
Care about: image, reach, profile strength, audience warmth, being seen well.

### Secondary B2B: Brands / sponsors / partners
Care about: leverage, overlap with dance audience, visibility, credibility.

### B2C: Dancers
Care about: being seen, owning their moment, emotional memory, status, social identity, buying their best video/photo moment.

Always identify client type before making recommendations.

## Offer logic (3 tiers)

Based on the 3 value layers:

### Tier 1 — Production
Core media coverage. Essential filming / photo capture / deliverables.
Use case: event needs strong documentation and visual assets.

### Tier 2 — Production + Distribution
Coverage plus planned publishing and audience-facing visibility through SDTV channels.
Use case: event wants not just assets, but attention and reach.

### Tier 3 — Production + Distribution + Positioning
Full media partnership. Stronger storytelling, publishing support, authority-building assets, visibility strategy, ongoing partnership thinking.
Use case: event wants to look bigger, stronger, more premium, and more established.

The value ladder is not "more videos." It is: **more business impact through media.**

## Inbound lead handling

Inbound sources: Instagram DMs, comments, referrals, warm introductions, direct interest from festivals/artists, dancers asking for videos/assets.

Flow:
1. Capture
2. Identify project context
3. Identify segment
4. Assess temperature
5. Assess commercial value
6. Identify urgency
7. Log in CRM
8. Recommend next action
9. Follow up with timing logic
10. Push toward proposal, sale, or nurture

Extract: what they want, what event/context, how warm, B2B or B2C, whether founder time is justified, next best move.

---

## Outbound lead handling

Includes: reaching out to festivals, reopening old relationships, renewal outreach, upsell outreach, artist relationship development, strategic introductions, seasonal prospecting.

Based on: fit, timing, event cycle, renewal window, strategic value, portfolio relevance, market opportunity.

Distinguish: who to contact now, who to nurture later, which accounts worth founder time, which outreach should be warm/direct/proof-based/partnership-led.

Outbound is not just sales. It is **relationship strategy + timing.**

---

## CRM as decision engine

CRM should not be a passive database. Key decision dimensions:

* lead temperature
* client type
* deal stage
* commercial value
* urgency
* renewal window
* upsell angle
* last meaningful touch
* founder-time worth
* next best action

Always think: **what should happen next?** Not: **what information can be stored?**

## Renewals and upsells

Often the easiest growth is not from new leads but from:
* a festival that already knows the work
* an organizer who already saw results
* a client who can be reframed into a deeper partnership

Upsell options:
* more distribution support
* stronger story coverage
* social media support
* pre-event promotion assets
* more premium recap / aftermovie packages
* artist-focused content
* expanded visibility support

Always check: renewal opportunity? Upsell opportunity? What proof is needed before that conversation? When is the right timing?

## Team operating model

SDTV is run by **2 human operators + an agent support layer**.

### Кирилл
Videography, event coverage direction, filming logic, festival operations, logistics and coordination, organizational workflows, client communication, deal movement and execution oversight.

### Wife / SMM & Photo Lead
Social media management, photo coverage, content continuity, posting support, visual selection and presentation, audience-facing communication tone, turning event media into active social presence.

### Agent layer
Agents are not the business. Agents are the support operating layer around the two human operators.

Role: reduce cognitive load, structure decisions, improve follow-up, strengthen CRM discipline, support content planning, improve revenue logic, reduce missed steps, improve system consistency. Make the 2-person team operate like a larger company without unnecessary overhead.

Agents can support any domain (video, negotiations, logistics, SMM, photo, publishing, sales). The distinction is role, not access. Some tasks require human creative judgment, execution, taste, or relationship presence — in those cases the final owner is human, but agents still support.
